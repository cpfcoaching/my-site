SOC 2 Type II Implementation Guide

Use this guide when an assessment item is missing, planned, implemented without enough operating history, or not supported by verified evidence. It tells the consultant what to implement, what evidence to retain, and when to stop.

Boundary: This guide supports readiness work. Management owns the system description, controls, risk decisions, and assertion. The CPA firm defines the examination approach and makes the audit conclusion.

Non-negotiable rules

Engagement rules and evidence expectations

1. Management owns the control. The consultant may design, configure, coach, test readiness, and organize evidence. A named management owner must approve the control and remain accountable for operation.
2. Do not backdate or manufacture evidence. If evidence does not exist, mark the control missing or implemented without verified evidence. Start clean operation and record the actual date.
3. Preserve exceptions. Do not replace failed samples, remove inconvenient records, or hide missed control occurrences. Record impact, owner, decision, correction, and closure proof.
4. Match the control to real work. A policy or control description must reflect the process that people and systems can repeat. Do not write an ideal process that the organization does not operate.
5. Use authoritative source evidence. Prefer system exports, approved records, tickets, logs, and signed review records. A screenshot needs the system, subject, date, actor, and relevant context.
6. Prove population completeness. Record the source, filters, time zone, date range, fields, extraction date, and reconciliation method for any population that may be sampled.
7. Retain evidence for the full observation period. One recent sample does not prove a recurring control operated throughout the period. Capture every occurrence required by the control cadence.
8. Separate readiness review from the audit conclusion. The consultant may label evidence ready for auditor review. Only the CPA firm determines sufficiency, sampling, exceptions, and the report opinion.
9. Control access to evidence. Store evidence in an approved location with least privilege, stable naming, retention, and change history. Remove secrets and unrelated personal data from working copies.
10. Escalate scope and independence conflicts. Send changes to scope, categories, subservice treatment, material commitments, and auditor independence questions to management and the CPA firm before implementation continues.

Operating method

Seven-step implementation workflow

1
Confirm applicability.
Link the item to scope, risk, commitments, criteria, and affected systems. Document why it applies or why management excludes it.
2
Write the control.
State who does what, in which system, when, how often, what evidence is created, and what happens when the control fails.
3
Approve the design.
Obtain the management owner, effective date, cadence, evidence location, and risk acceptance for known constraints.
4
Implement and train.
Configure the workflow, assign access, publish the procedure, and train every operator and reviewer.
5
Dry test.
Run one trace from trigger through approval, execution, evidence capture, exception handling, and management review.
6
Operate and collect.
Run the control at its real cadence. Keep the population and every required occurrence across the observation period.
7
Verify and hand off.
Test evidence against the control, log exceptions, obtain owner sign-off, and index the evidence for the CPA firm.
Evidence quality gate

Exit criteria for “Verified evidence”

  • The control owner and operator are named.
  • The control wording matches actual operation.
  • The effective date and cadence are recorded.
  • The source system and evidence location are controlled.
  • Evidence identifies the subject, actor, reviewer, date, and result.
  • The full population is reproducible when sampling may apply.
  • Evidence exists for the required occurrences in the period.
  • Exceptions remain in the record and have dispositions.
  • Changes to the control are versioned and dated.
  • The owner completed a readiness review and sign-off.
Stop condition: If any item fails, keep the assessment status at Implemented or lower. Record the exact missing proof and the next valid control occurrence that can produce it.
Never “fix” history: Do not backdate approval, recreate a meeting record, alter a system export, or perform a control today and claim it operated earlier.
Foundational

Scope and system description

Implementation ruleSet the smallest defensible boundary that covers the service commitments and risks. Identify infrastructure, software, people, procedures, data, locations, and material subservice organizations.
Consultant procedureInterview service owners. Trace customer data. Reconcile the architecture, asset, vendor, and contract inventories. Draft the boundary and change log for management approval.
Expected evidenceApproved scope statement, system description, diagrams, data flows, asset and vendor inventories, commitment register, boundary decisions, and dated change history.
Exit criteriaEvery material component has an owner and boundary decision. Documents agree with production. Management approves the description. Open CPA questions are recorded.

Cadence expectation: review at least annually and after a material product, infrastructure, location, vendor, data, or commitment change.

CC1, CC2, CC3, CC4, CC5, CC9

Governance and risk management

Implementation ruleAssign accountability, communicate requirements, assess risks, operate monitoring, and correct control deficiencies through a documented governance process.
Consultant procedureBuild the responsibility matrix, risk register, policy register, control inventory, exception log, and management review agenda. Coach owners but do not accept risk for them.
Expected evidenceApproved policies, risk assessment, treatment decisions, meeting minutes, metrics, acknowledgements, training records, findings, remediation tickets, and closure approvals.
Exit criteriaPolicies and risks have owners and review dates. Management decisions are recorded. Findings trace to closure. Recurring oversight occurred at the defined cadence.

Illustrative cadence: annual policy and risk review, plus periodic management oversight and event-driven updates. Management and the CPA firm must confirm the actual cadence.

CC6

Logical and physical access

Implementation ruleAuthorize access before provision. Limit privilege. Protect authentication. Remove access promptly. Review access using complete, current system populations.
Consultant procedureInventory identity sources and privileged roles. Define joiner, mover, leaver triggers. Configure approvals and MFA. Run a complete access review and trace removals to completion.
Expected evidenceRequests, approvals, account records, MFA coverage, termination timestamps, role definitions, privileged-user lists, review decisions, removal tickets, and physical-access records.
Exit criteriaSamples trace end to end. Terminations meet the approved target. Review populations reconcile to systems. Reviewer decisions and completed removals are visible.

Cadence expectation: lifecycle controls operate per event. Access reviews follow the approved risk-based cadence. The control description must define timing and escalation.

CC8

Change management and secure development

Implementation ruleProduction changes must be authorized, tested, traceable, and recoverable. Emergency changes need defined approval and timely retrospective review.
Consultant procedureMap repositories, pipelines, cloud consoles, and ticketing. Enforce reviews and protected deployment paths. Define compensating review where small-team separation is not possible.
Expected evidenceComplete change population, tickets, pull requests, tests, approvals, deployment records, security checks, rollback notes, emergency reason, and follow-up review.
Exit criteriaProduction changes reconcile to an approved source. Samples include all required steps. Bypass paths are restricted and monitored. Emergency exceptions are reviewed.

Cadence expectation: per production change, with recurring review of emergency access, bypasses, and failed deployments.

CC7

Security operations

Implementation ruleMaintain current assets. Detect security events and vulnerabilities. Assign response based on risk. Verify remediation and keep approved exceptions visible.
Consultant procedureReconcile inventories. Define logging and alert coverage. Set risk-based remediation targets. Establish ticketing, exception approval, escalation, and closure verification.
Expected evidenceInventories, scan populations, findings, alerts, triage records, response tickets, remediation proof, exception approvals, coverage reports, log retention settings, and trend reviews.
Exit criteriaCoverage matches scope. Findings reconcile to source populations. Deadlines and escalation are testable. Closure has technical verification, not only a closed ticket.

Cadence expectation: monitoring runs continuously or at the defined frequency. Review and remediation timing must match risk, commitments, and approved policy.

CC7, A1

Incident response and resilience

Implementation ruleDefine authority, severity, escalation, communication, evidence handling, recovery objectives, and lessons learned before an incident occurs.
Consultant procedureBuild the contact and decision matrix. Validate legal, privacy, insurer, customer, and vendor paths. Run a tabletop and a restoration test. Assign and close findings.
Expected evidenceApproved plans, contact validation, training, tabletop records, incident population, tickets, communications, post-incident review, backup monitoring, restore tests, and recovery results.
Exit criteriaRoles can execute without improvising. Tests meet defined objectives or create tracked gaps. Restore proof shows usable recovery. Findings trace to closure and retest.

Illustrative cadence: at least annual testing and after material change. Backup, restore, recovery, and notification cadences must reflect system and contractual needs.

CC9

Vendor and subservice management

Implementation ruleIdentify material vendors, assess risk before use, establish required terms, monitor changes, and operate complementary user entity controls.
Consultant procedureReconcile procurement, finance, identity, cloud, and architecture sources. Tier vendors. Review current reports and bridge letters. Track findings, renewals, and control dependencies.
Expected evidenceVendor inventory, tier rationale, due diligence, contracts, reports, bridge letters, complementary control mapping, findings, approvals, exceptions, monitoring, and termination records.
Exit criteriaMaterial vendors have owners and current reviews. Report periods are understood. Complementary controls have internal owners. Contract and finding exceptions are approved.

Cadence expectation: before onboarding, at renewal or defined risk-based intervals, and after material service or security changes.

Operating effectiveness

Type II evidence management

Implementation ruleKeep a control matrix and evidence index that show who operated each control, when it operated, which population applies, what result occurred, and how exceptions were handled.
Consultant procedureInventory control frequencies. Create an evidence calendar. Test exports before the period. Review evidence after each occurrence. Keep an immutable exception record and readiness sign-off.
Expected evidenceControl matrix, evidence calendar, population specifications, complete exports, samples, evidence index, exception log, change history, owner attestations, and management readiness review.
Exit criteriaAll required occurrences are indexed. Populations can be reproduced. Evidence matches the control and period. Exceptions are dispositioned. Management approves auditor handoff.

The CPA firm decides final samples and sufficiency. The consultant should prepare complete populations and representative readiness samples without predicting or influencing auditor selections.

Handoff

Consultant completion package

  • Approved scope, categories, boundaries, and subservice treatment.
  • Current system description and dated change log.
  • Risk-to-criteria-to-control matrix with owners and frequencies.
  • Evidence request list, evidence index, and controlled repository map.
  • Population definitions and reproducible export instructions.
  • Exception log with impact, decision, action, and closure status.
  • Readiness test results with failures clearly identified.
  • Management approvals, residual-risk decisions, and open issues.
  • Questions for the CPA firm, with no assumed audit conclusions.
  • Operating calendar that continues through the end of the observation period.
Completion rule: The consultant is done only when management can operate the controls without consultant dependency, reproduce the evidence, explain the exceptions, and maintain the cadence.
